# Dungeons and raids

Bots never go into a dungeon by themselves. You send them, on the **Dungeons** tab (or with the order **Dungeon** in a
bot's Orders sub-tab, which leads that bot's squad in).

## The dungeon run window

The **Dungeons** tab takes you through four steps; the list on the right shows the runs that are going.

### 1. The dungeon

Choose a dungeon or a raid. Your guild plays the dungeons **in their order** (by level): at the start only the first
dungeon of your faction's order is open, and a run that brings its last boss down opens the next one for good. A locked
dungeon says which one to finish first. The raids are at the end of the list and always open.

### 2. The group

Click the bots of the group, up to five (up to 40 for a raid). The list shows each bot's level, role and readiness
(how well its gear fits the dungeon). There are no level limits: an over-levelled bot is welcome, a low one is shown so
you can decide.

- A tank and a healer by their dungeon role ([Talents, roles and combat](talents-roles-combat.md#dungeon-roles)) make the
  run safe; the first tank leads the group.
- Your own character is the first row: see [Playing along](#playing-along).
- For a raid, **Whole guild** puts you and every bot that is in the world and not on another run into it.

### 3. Preparation

Nothing here has to be changed.

- **Talents for the run:** per bot, as it levels, or tank or healer with a talent template (this sets its dungeon role).
- **From the guild storage:** every elixir, flask, scroll, sharpening stone, oil, poison and scope the storage holds is
  ticked; click a kind to keep it out of the run. Each character uses only what fits it (one battle and one guardian
  elixir and one scroll, the best for its role; what its weapons take), once at the start of a run and once after a
  wipe. Reserves you set in the storage are kept.
- **How it fights:** how long the others wait while the tank takes the creatures, below which health the healer heals
  during that wait, from how many creatures the tank pulls carefully, whether everybody turns to a creature that runs
  away.

### 4. How often, and how

- **How often:** 1 to 10 runs, or until you stop it.
- **How much it fights on its way:**
  - *Bosses:* only what stands in the way to the bosses;
  - *Clear:* every room on the way;
  - *Clear in big pulls:* rooms in bigger pulls (you set how far and how many);
  - *Tribute run* (Dire Maul North only): the group frees King Gordok's guards instead of killing them and gets their
    tribute and buffs (see below).

**Start**: the group is taken into the dungeon from wherever its bots are. Tanks and healers change to their role at the
entrance. Thirty seconds after the last boss the group leaves to each bot's hearthstone place. With more runs it
repairs where it stands, empties its bags into the storage and goes into a fresh instance. The game lets a character
into five different instances an hour; a group that farms faster waits for the next.

The list of running runs has **Last run** (finish this run, then stop) and **Stop**.

## How a dungeon group plays

- The tank leads along the way through the dungeon, the others follow close behind it.
- At a pull the others hold back until the tank has the creatures; the healer waits too, unless somebody is in danger.
  Once the fight is on, it heals as needed and spends no mana on damage.
- The tank pulls carefully after a wipe and for big groups at its level; a member under attack gets help first.
- The group rests together, and waits until everybody is ready before a boss or a big pull. A boss's room is cleared
  before the boss.
- Bosses that an event or an object brings are played: talks, escorts, gongs, levers, altars.
- The dead are brought back after a fight. Two wipes end a run (five in a raid).
- **Loot:** bots roll on what is an upgrade for them; everything else goes into the guild storage through the loot
  filter.

## Playing along

Pick your own character in the group step and it comes along as a member; a bot always leads.

- **Played by hand:** the group goes its pace and waits for you when you fall behind, rest, fight or die; a dead
  character is offered a resurrection. The run's teleports take you along (in, after a wipe, out).
- **Watch:** the AI plays your character like any bot of the group. **Take over** gives it back to you at any time.

When the run ends while you are in a fight, your character follows the group out once the fight is over.

## The Dire Maul tribute run

In Dire Maul North the tribute run keeps King Gordok's six guards alive and kills only the king, which crowns your group
and gives every member the guards' buffs and the tribute chest. The group needs materials from the guild storage for
the guards' tasks (the window says which ones are missing; the shopping list and craft orders can get them). The inner
door is opened by a rogue with high lockpicking, an engineer with a seaforium charge or a blacksmith with a skeleton key
of the storage, otherwise one guard's key is taken from him.

## Raids

The raids of level 60: **Molten Core**, **Blackwing Lair** (through Upper Blackrock Spire), **Zul'Gurub**, the **Ruins
of Ahn'Qiraj** and the **Temple of Ahn'Qiraj**.

- A raid group holds up to 40 characters. It wants several tanks (the first leads) and a healer for every four or five
  others, by their dungeon roles, and the mode *Clear*.
- A raid saves its progress for the week: a raid run goes into the saved instance and counts what fell this week. One run
  per order.
- The raid buffs need reagents (Sacred Candles, Symbols of Kings, Wild Thornroot, Arcane Powder); keep them in the
  storage, for example with the shopping list.
- The raids want epic gear; in rare gear early bosses wipe the raid.

State of the raids: Molten Core, Zul'Gurub, the Ruins and every boss of Blackwing Lair have been done by a raid of 40
bots in tests. In the Temple of Ahn'Qiraj seven of nine bosses have fallen; Viscidus and C'Thun are not beaten yet.
